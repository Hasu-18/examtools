## What are IBPS PO and IBPS Clerk

IBPS, the Institute of Banking Personnel Selection, runs two separate Common Recruitment Processes (CRP) each year for India's public sector banks. **CRP PO/MT** recruits Probationary Officers and Management Trainees, the officer entry point. **CRP CSA**, still widely called IBPS Clerk, recruits Customer Service Associates, the clerical entry point. Both are conducted by IBPS on behalf of the same pool of participating banks, but they are entirely separate exams with separate notifications, patterns and cut-offs.

For 2026 the cycles are CRP PO/MT-XVI and CRP CSA-XVI, the latter recruiting against indicative vacancies for financial year 2027-28. Clearing either exam does not by itself guarantee a job: candidates are provisionally allotted to one participating bank based on merit-cum-preference and actual vacancies, and the bank itself has the final say. For CSA, the official notification states this plainly: the CRP's validity "will automatically expire on the day which is one year after the date of Provisional Allotment or until a fresh provisional allotment is made, whichever is earlier." There is no lifetime certificate, unlike a teacher-eligibility test.

The 11 participating banks under CRP CSA-XVI are Bank of Baroda, Bank of India, Bank of Maharashtra, Canara Bank, Central Bank of India, Indian Bank, Indian Overseas Bank, Punjab National Bank, Punjab & Sind Bank, UCO Bank and Union Bank of India (Union Bank's vacancy cells were marked "NR" throughout the annexure, meaning not reported at notification stage). PO recruitment draws on a similar set of participating banks, though the list is not always identical to the Clerk list.

PO figures above are as commonly published for recent CRP PO/MT cycles; confirm them against the PO/MT-XVI notification before you rely on them.

Clerk vacancies are filled State/UT-wise, so a candidate applies against one specific State or UT and is examined there (subject to reallocation for administrative reasons, without affecting which State's vacancies they're considered against). PO recruitment is all-India with no such State-linkage.

## Selection process

**IBPS Clerk (confirmed):** Online Preliminary Exam (qualifying only, sectional cut-offs apply) → Online Main Exam (the only score used for final merit) → Local Language Proficiency Test, qualifying and conducted after Mains but before joining → provisional allotment by merit-cum-preference. There is no interview.

**IBPS PO (reported):** Online Preliminary Exam (qualifying) → Online Main Exam (objective plus a descriptive paper) → Personality Test and an Interview stage (typically 100 marks) → provisional allotment, with Mains and Interview combined into final merit.

## Syllabus

Both exams draw on the same broad areas, at slightly different difficulty for PO. IBPS itself does not publish an exhaustive topic list, only these section names, the detail below is the standard preparation scope used across coaching sources.

- **English Language:** reading comprehension, cloze test, error spotting, sentence correction and rearrangement, para jumbles, vocabulary, grammar
- **Quantitative Aptitude / Numerical Ability:** simplification, number series, percentage, ratio, profit and loss, interest, time and work, mensuration, data interpretation (tables, bar/line/pie charts, caselets)
- **Reasoning Ability:** puzzles, seating arrangement, syllogism, coding-decoding, blood relations, direction sense, inequality, data sufficiency
- **Computer Aptitude:** fundamentals, hardware/software, internet and networking, MS Office, digital banking (a standalone Mains section for PO; folded into Reasoning-related content for Clerk)
- **General/Financial/Banking Awareness:** current affairs, RBI and monetary policy, banking terminology, government schemes, static GK
- **Descriptive (PO Mains only):** essay and comprehension writing in English

## What the score is used for

Neither exam produces a portable, reusable score. A PO or Clerk Prelims score only qualifies a candidate for that cycle's Mains; the Prelims score itself carries no weight in final merit. Mains score (plus Interview, for PO) is used purely for that year's provisional allotment, and the whole process lapses one year after allotment or on the next fresh allotment, whichever comes first. A candidate who wants another shot at a different bank or a higher vacancy count has to apply again in the next annual CRP cycle, at whatever age and eligibility rules that year's notification sets.
